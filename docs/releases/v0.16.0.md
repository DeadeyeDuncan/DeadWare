- **Connections history saves one row per minute.** Each minute is saved as one row per application and destination shortly after it ends (2 seconds plus one to two polling intervals, 3–4 seconds at the default 1-second polling), about 60 times fewer rows than 0.15 at 1-second polling, and the number of rows no longer depends on the polling interval. Idle and light use keep the full 30 days; a steady 20 application–destination pairs a minute fill the 256 MiB cap in about 8–9 days. Rows saved by earlier versions stay until they are 30 days old or evicted, and `connections.db` does not shrink (freed space is reused). History and CSV export show a minute a few seconds after it ends. A crash or power loss can lose about the last minute, instead of about 15 seconds. A small backward clock correction (up to 60 seconds) no longer loses connection history. A 7- or 30-day History view on a full file takes a few seconds while "Reading saved history…" shows; an interval that holds too many saved rows to group says "This interval holds too many saved rows to show. Choose a shorter interval." and shows History as unavailable, never as an empty period.
- **Unlocated counts match Live.** History's unlocated count no longer includes local or special-purpose peers: like Live, it counts only public addresses with no map position.
- **Traffic strip.** A stacked chart of the six applications with the most traffic, plus Other, as average rates per slot, sits under the map on Connections **History** and replaces the share bar in Usage history's **Applications** card. Its colours identify applications, not direction. Time that was not recorded is hatched, never drawn as zero; the minute not yet saved stays empty and its hover says "Not saved yet". On Connections, clicking the strip (or Left, Right and Enter on it) moves the minute cursor, and clicking an application in its legend shows only that application, with an **Application: … ✕** button that clears the filter. On Usage history, the strip shares the cursor of the CPU, RAM and GPU charts, a click or Enter lists that slot in the Applications table, and **Show in Connections** brings the dashboard forward on Connections History for the listed slot or the period (the last 30 days at most), showing only the selected application when one is selected.
- **Usage gap notice and "Capacity shortened retention".** The Applications card's notice now reads "{n} recorded minutes had no or incomplete per-application capture; the totals may miss some of their traffic, and the strip leaves them out." Connections' "Capacity shortened retention" now shows only while the 256 MiB cap has removed minutes the 30-day retention would still keep; once those minutes would have expired anyway, it is no longer shown. An interval the file no longer keeps shows a "Not kept" band and "Not kept before …" under the strip.
- **Line charts.** Usage history's line charts, including Application resources, now connect fully recorded slots that hold one minute start fewer than their length; before, a fully recorded slot on a fractional grid could show as a gap.
- **Destinations table.** Connections lists every destination the filters allow, in Live and History, one row per remote address, including the local, special-purpose and unlocated ones the map cannot draw. **Show** All, Internet or Local narrows the table and Export destinations only, and is remembered. Each scope lists its top 1,000 by traffic, with exact counts in the count line. Rows update in place and keep their order while the pointer rests on the table, while its menu is open and for 3 seconds after a navigation key; F5 re-sorts. Selecting a row highlights its nodes on the map. Ctrl+C or **Copy row** copies the row and **Copy address** the address; **Export destinations…** writes every destination in the scope for the History interval to a new CSV. In History the table follows the minute cursor and lists saved minutes.
- **Returning to 0.15.** 0.15 keeps reading `connections.db` and records one row per poll again; History shows both kinds of rows. It goes back to its own Unlocated count, which includes local peers. It ignores the saved Destinations scope, and returning to 0.16 opens the table on All. Usage history and Application resources files are unchanged by 0.16.
