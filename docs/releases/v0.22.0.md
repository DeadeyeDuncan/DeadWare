## 0.22.0 — Mouse and everyday

- **Everyday card.** A new **Everyday** card on the **Lighting** page, under the desk, has the **Lights on/off shortcut** (`Ctrl + Alt + L` to start, changed with **Change**, removed with **No shortcut**), **Off when the PC sits unused** (1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes) and **Off when the PC is locked**. The shortcut, the **Turn lights off** / **Turn lights on** line in the tray menu and the **Lights on** switch all flip the same saved switch, so lights you turn off stay off across a restart.
- **Idle and lock are temporary.** They don't touch the **Lights on** switch. While one of them, or the PC waking from sleep, has the lights dark, a yellow line at the top of the page says why, and the pictures show no lights. A press of the shortcut within about two seconds of an idle spell ending only brings the lights back.
- **One card for the Aerox 9.** **Mouse · SteelSeries Aerox 9** covers the cable and the wireless receiver, with one set of colours. The first time the mouse is seen on its receiver, the cable's colours are copied across once; the old entry stays.
- **Battery.** A **Battery** section shows a bar, the percentage and, on the cable, whether it is charging. It is read while you use the mouse on wireless, at most once a minute, and every 10 seconds on the cable. There is no low-battery warning.
- **Mouse sleep time.** **Mouse sleeps after** sends the mouse a sleep time, from 1 to 20 minutes or **Never**, as soon as you pick it. With one chosen, the mouse's lights hold steady while it lies still so it can sleep, and moving it brings the effect back. **Leave as the mouse is** stops DeadWare sending a time but can't restore the mouse's own earlier one.
- **The cable makes the lights blink.** Plugging in the mouse's cable makes every light blink for a few seconds while OpenRGB looks for hardware again, at most once a minute.
- **PC sleep and wake.** DeadWare sends the lights nothing while the PC sleeps, and sends every light its colours again about 10 seconds after it wakes.
- **Reads no input.** The shortcut is a Windows hotkey, idle time is Windows' own idle time and mouse use is the pointer's position. DeadWare adds no keyboard or mouse hook and reads nothing you type.
- **Nothing changes until you use it.** With the Everyday card untouched and no sleep time chosen, the lights and the saved file are exactly as in 0.21.0, apart from the mouse's single card.
- **Going back to 0.21.0.** If you change the Everyday card or pick a mouse sleep time, `lighting.json` becomes version 4. 0.21.0 reads it and runs the lights, but won't save lighting changes over it; what you set is there again when you update. The cable mouse's old colours are still in the file.
