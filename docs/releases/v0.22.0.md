## 0.22.0 — Mouse and everyday

- **Everyday card.** A new **Everyday** card on the **Lighting** page, under the desk, has the **Lights on/off shortcut** (`Ctrl + Alt + L` to start, changed with **Change**, removed with **No shortcut**), **Off when the PC sits unused** (1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes) and **Off when the PC is locked**. The shortcut, the **Turn lights off** / **Turn lights on** line in the tray menu and the **Lights on** switch all flip the same saved switch, so lights you turn off stay off across a restart. The shortcut and the lock, sleep and wake handling are built to work but have not yet been tried on a real PC.
- **Idle and lock are temporary.** They don't touch the **Lights on** switch. While one of them, or the PC waking from sleep, has the lights dark, a yellow line at the top of the page says why, and the pictures show no lights. A press of the shortcut within about two seconds of an idle spell ending only brings the lights back.
- **One card for the Aerox 9.** **Mouse · SteelSeries Aerox 9** covers the cable and the wireless receiver, with one set of colours. The cable mouse's old colours are copied to the new card once, if it has none of its own; the old entry stays.
- **Battery.** A **Battery** section shows a bar, the percentage and, on the cable, whether it is charging. It is read while you use the mouse on wireless, at most once a minute, and every 10 seconds on the cable. There is no low-battery warning.
- **Mouse sleep time.** **Mouse sleeps after** saves a sleep time, from 1 to 20 minutes or **Never**, as soon as you pick it, and sends it to the mouse over the receiver (at once if the mouse is awake). With one chosen and delivered over the receiver, the mouse's lights hold steady while it lies still so it can sleep, and moving it brings the effect back. **Leave as the mouse is** stops DeadWare sending a time but can't restore the mouse's own earlier one.
- **The cable may make the lights blink.** Plugging in the mouse's cable, when OpenRGB hasn't already found it, may make all lights blink for a few seconds while OpenRGB looks for hardware again, at most once a minute. The mouse should then appear on its cable. This has not yet been tried with the real mouse.
- **PC sleep and wake.** DeadWare sends the lights nothing while the PC sleeps, and is meant to send the lights their colours again about 10 seconds after it wakes. Not yet tried on a real PC: the shortcut from another program, lock, sleep and wake, the wireless mouse and the cable rescan. The hardware check will confirm them.
- **No new input reading.** The shortcut is a Windows global shortcut, idle time is Windows' own last-input time and mouse use is the pointer's position. Nothing new watches the keyboard or mouse as you use them (the key-press effects from 0.21.0 still do, only while one is in use).
- **Off until you choose it, except the shortcut.** While DeadWare runs, **Ctrl + Alt + L** turns the lights off and on in every program from the first start, so another program that uses those keys inside its own window stops receiving them, with no warning (the red line appears only when another program already holds the keys for the whole of Windows). **No shortcut** on the Everyday card frees them. Idle, lock and the mouse sleep time stay off until you choose them, and until then the saved file is as in 0.21.0, apart from the mouse's colours, now saved once for both connections.
- **Going back to 0.21.0.** If you change the Everyday card or pick a mouse sleep time, `lighting.json` becomes version 4. 0.21.0 reads it and runs the lights, but won't save lighting changes over it; what you set is there again when you update. The cable mouse's old colours are still in the file.
