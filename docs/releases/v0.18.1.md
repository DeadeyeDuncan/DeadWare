## 0.18.1 — Lighter, roomier, and the colour dots are back

- **Much less processor time, especially on long graph ranges.** Every graph used to rebuild itself from its whole range twice a second at 0.5-second polling; at six hours that is 43,200 readings per line, each time. A graph now rebuilds when time has moved it by a pixel, or when something about it changes. Measured on one PC (Ryzen 7 7700X, running as administrator, 0.5-second polling), DeadWare's processor time per minute fell from 16.1 to 4.9 seconds with only the widget showing a six-hour range, from 14.9 to 7.5 seconds with the widget at one minute, and from 33.3 to 5.5 seconds with the dashboard open on Overview at six hours, each compared with this release before these changes. On the same PC, 0.18.0 used 51 and 107 seconds per minute after a few hours.
- **Memory no longer climbs for six hours.** The graph history kept a full copy of every reading, hundreds of values twice a second, which grew to about half a gigabyte at six hours; 0.18.0 reached 1 GB on one PC. It now stores each value compactly, about 52 MB for six hours of 300 values at 0.5-second polling, and lets go of the saved-graph file it reads at start-up once that history is restored.
- **Long-range graphs stand still between moves.** Where a reading would move a graph by less than half a pixel (15 minutes and longer at 0.5-second polling on a typical card), or while Windows animation effects are off, the graph moves on a pixel at a time instead of sliding, and costs nothing in between. The shorter ranges still slide.
- **The Connections map rests when nobody is looking.** Its animation runs at about 30 frames a second while you use it, slows to 15 after a minute without input and to 10 after five, and stops while it is hidden, minimised, scrolled away or covered by other windows, or while Windows animation effects are off. The oceans, land, coasts and borders are drawn once and reused, and each arc is prepared once instead of every frame. The Connections page no longer works while the dashboard sits in the tray, and its Destinations table builds only the rows on screen. With only the widget and its live map showing, the map change alone took DeadWare from 27 to 18.5 seconds of processor time per minute on the PC measured above.
- **Lighter readings.** Network totals read only the adapters' counters each poll, and disk throughput comes from one query instead of three per drive. The drive scan that paused readings for about a second once a minute runs in the background. Per-app GPU counters are read every poll while the Processes page is open and every 5 seconds otherwise. With **Extended sensors**, motherboard, memory and controller readings update every 2 seconds, apart from the chip with the CPU pump, and drive readings come from the 30-second storage read: on the Sensors page their rows refresh at those rates, and with **Storage sensors** off there are no drive rows. Free space on the Storage page now follows within 10 seconds.
- **The colour dots are back on Overview.** Each graph card has a dot per trace beside its pop-out button, as in 0.17: the GPU fan and CPU pump dots show while their lines do, and drive cards share the Disk card's colours. A dot opens the trace's colour and line style; picking a colour closes it. While per-core CPU usage is shown, the CPU dot also lists each core's line and the actions that copy the CPU line's style to every core. **Settings → Appearance → Graph lines** works as before.
- **More room for the graphs.** The sidebar is 220 pixels wide instead of 320. The page header is shorter and carries Overview's graphics card choice, history range and **Open widget**, so Overview has no toolbar row of its own. Each card puts its device line beside the title, its MIN, AVG and MAX beside a smaller reading, the GPU fan or CPU pump on that same row, and the network rates on one row. Graphs draw their top label inside the plot and use smaller labels, which also gives the widget, pop-outs and Storage page more plot.
- **Drives are named by letter.** A drive reads "C: Samsung SSD 980 PRO 1TB" wherever DeadWare names it: the Overview and Storage cards, drive pop-outs, graph readouts and Cooling Health. Drives in USB enclosures lose Windows' "SCSI Disk Device" suffix.
- **Windows keep one colour.** Mica took its tint from the desktop wallpaper while a window had focus and turned plain grey without it, so DeadWare's windows changed colour as focus moved. They now keep the plain background, and the pop-out and widget title bars match it.
- **CPU temperature on laptops and tablets without administrator rights or PawnIO.** Intel and AMD CPU temperatures need DeadWare running as administrator with the PawnIO driver, and ARM processors such as a Snapdragon Surface's have no sensor DeadWare can read, so many laptops and tablets showed no CPU temperature. A device with a battery now falls back to Windows' thermal zones: a zone named for the CPU, otherwise the hottest. The status line marks the reading approximate and the zones appear on the Sensors page. Desktops are left out, because their boards often report fixed placeholder zones.
- **The GPU fan and CPU pump lines get their colours.** They drew in the accent colour; they now default to the amber and blue that Settings describes.
- **The widget and pop-out graphs keep their size on a second monitor.** With monitors at different scales (for example a 150% main screen and a 100% second screen), a widget or pop-out saved on the other screen reopened smaller each time DeadWare started: two thirds of its size from 150% to 100%, and that smaller size was then saved. Windows rescales a new window as it is moved onto a screen with another scale; DeadWare now applies the saved size again once the window is there. A window that 0.18.0 already shrank stays as it is until you resize it.
- **Saved graph history no longer stops when its file is full.** The six-hour graph history is kept in a file limited to 64 MiB. At 0.5-second polling with several drives and fans, six hours do not fit (one PC filled it in under five), and once full DeadWare showed "Graph history unavailable (SqliteException)" and saved nothing more until old readings aged out and it was restarted. It now drops the oldest saved readings to make room, and the status reads "Graph history saved locally · the newest hours that fit its 64 MiB file". The graphs on screen were never affected while DeadWare stayed open; what was lost was the history shown after a restart. This also affected 0.17 and earlier.
