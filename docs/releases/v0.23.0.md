## 0.23.0 — The hub

- **The switch.** **Settings → Lighting** has a new heading, **Corsair iCUE LINK hub**, with a switch, **DeadWare drives the hub**. With it on, DeadWare lights the Corsair iCUE LINK System Hub itself and reads its coolant temperature and its fan and pump speeds. DeadWare never sets fan or pump speeds. A state line under the switch says where things stand.
- **A checked take-over.** Turning the switch on opens **Let DeadWare drive the Corsair hub?** with the speeds the hub showed just now, and **Cancel** is the default. OpenRGB restarts without the hub, so all lights blink once, and DeadWare takes the hub. About 15 seconds later it reads the speeds again; if the pump or a fan runs more than 10% slower than the dialog showed, or the pump stops, DeadWare gives the hub back to its own built-in settings, says why and leaves the choice to you with **Back to OpenRGB, as before** and **Try the take-over again**. Before each take it checks again for iCUE, the Corsair services, DeadLight and another OpenRGB.
- **The hand-back.** With the switch on, **Quit DeadWare**, turning lighting off, **Update & restart**, **Roll back & restart** and a sign-out give the hub back first, so it returns to its own stored lights and fan settings. DeadWare also lets go of the hub, and takes it again by itself, whenever iCUE, the Corsair service, DeadLight or another OpenRGB needs it, or the hub goes missing or silent. While the PC sleeps DeadWare sends the hub nothing, and takes it again about 10 seconds after waking. With the switch off nothing is handed back, as in 0.22.0.
- **Readings and the coolant line.** The hub's card on the **Lighting** page gets a **Readings** section, and the Sensors page gets one row per reading under "Corsair iCUE LINK hub": coolant, pump and each fan (five on a hub with three fans and a pump; the pump shows twice on a PC whose motherboard reads it too, on purpose). **Show coolant temperature on CPU temperature graphs**, under **Settings → Appearance** and unticked to start, adds the coolant as a dashed line. Cooling Health doesn't use coolant temperature, and its two messages now say that coolant and ambient temperatures "are not part of this comparison".
- **iCUE, DeadLight and Retire.** **Settings → Lighting** shows whether iCUE is running, whether the Corsair services run or start with Windows and whether iCUE starts at sign-in, with the steps to switch each off; and, when DeadLight is found, whether it runs and starts at sign-in. **Retire DeadLight** switches off its start-up task and stops it and the OpenRGB it started, and deletes nothing. DeadWare shows these facts and never changes iCUE.
- **One identity.** The hub's colours, tuning and place are kept under one entry whichever way it is lit; the first time, its colours are copied once from the old OpenRGB entry, which stays for 0.22.0. A hub with a part DeadWare doesn't know, or more than one hub, is refused with the reason, and the front three fans on the Lighting Node Pro stay as they are.
- **Nothing changes until the switch is on.** With it off, the hub is lit through OpenRGB as in 0.22.0, and the saved lighting file is written as 0.22.0 writes it.
- **Not yet tried on a real PC.** Nothing in this release has been tried on a real PC yet; the owner's hardware check follows. These are not part of that check and are covered by tests only: iCUE starting while DeadWare drives the hub, a failed speed check, the stopped-pump rule, a stuck USB call, Retire with DeadLight running, a hub with unknown parts.
- **Going back to 0.22.0.** With the switch on, `lighting.json` is version 5, which 0.22.0 reads and runs the lights from but won't save lighting changes over; switch the hub off before going back. DeadWare writes OpenRGB's hub detector back on whenever it stops, but if 0.22.0 shows an unlit hub (the helper was ended, not stopped), start and quit 0.23.0 once.
