## 0.23.0 — The hub

- **The switch.** **Settings → Lighting** has a new heading, **Corsair iCUE LINK hub**, with a switch, **DeadWare drives the hub**. With it on, DeadWare lights the Corsair iCUE LINK System Hub itself and reads its coolant temperature and its fan and pump speeds. DeadWare never sets fan or pump speeds. A state line under the switch says where things stand.
- **A checked take-over.** Turning the switch on opens **Let DeadWare drive the Corsair hub?** with the speeds the hub showed just now, and **Cancel** is the default. OpenRGB restarts without the hub, so all lights blink once, and DeadWare takes the hub. About 15 seconds later it reads the speeds again; if the pump or a fan runs more than 10% slower than the dialog showed, or the pump stops, DeadWare gives the hub back to its own built-in settings, says why and leaves the choice to you with **Back to OpenRGB, as before** and **Try the take-over again**. Before each take it checks again for iCUE, the Corsair services, DeadLight and another OpenRGB.
- **The hand-back.** With the switch on, **Quit DeadWare**, turning lighting off, **Update & restart**, **Roll back & restart**, choosing another OpenRGB, **Restart as administrator** and a sign-out give the hub back first, so it returns to its own stored lights and fan settings. DeadWare also lets go of the hub, and takes it again by itself, whenever iCUE, the Corsair service, DeadLight or another OpenRGB needs it, the hub goes missing or silent, or a second iCUE LINK hub is plugged in. While the PC sleeps DeadWare sends the hub nothing, and takes it again about 10 seconds after waking. With the switch off nothing is handed back, as in 0.22.0.
- **Readings and the coolant line.** The hub's card on the **Lighting** page gets a **Readings** section, and the Sensors page gets one row per reading under "Corsair iCUE LINK hub": coolant, pump and each fan (five on a hub with three fans and a pump; the pump shows twice on a PC whose motherboard reads it too, on purpose). **Show coolant temperature on CPU temperature graphs**, under **Settings → Appearance** and unticked to start, adds the coolant as a dashed line. Cooling Health doesn't use coolant temperature, and its two messages now say that coolant and ambient temperatures "are not part of this comparison".
- **iCUE, DeadLight and Retire.** **Settings → Lighting** shows whether iCUE is running, whether the Corsair services run or start with Windows and whether iCUE starts at sign-in, with the steps to switch each off; and, when DeadLight is found, whether it runs and starts at sign-in. **Retire DeadLight** switches off its start-up task and stops it and the OpenRGB it started, and deletes nothing. It needs DeadWare running as administrator; it stops nothing when it can't confirm which folder is DeadLight's, and it leaves DeadWare's own OpenRGB running when that one is the one in DeadLight's folder. DeadWare shows these facts and never changes iCUE.
- **One identity.** The hub's colours, tuning and place are kept under one entry whichever way it is lit; the first time, its colours are copied once from the old OpenRGB entry, which stays for 0.22.0. With more than one hub listed the copy is still made, from one of the old hub entries. A hub with a part DeadWare doesn't know, or more than one hub, is refused with the reason, and the front three fans on the Lighting Node Pro stay as they are.
- **Little changes until the switch is on.** With it off, the hub is lit through OpenRGB as in 0.22.0, and the saved lighting file keeps a form 0.22.0 can save over, apart from one thing: in every mode, the hub's colours are copied once under a new name and saved there from then on. The old entry stays, so after going back to 0.22.0 the hub shows its colours from before the update.
- **What has been tried on a real PC.** On the owner's PC, in two sittings on 27 November 2026 with test builds of this version: taking the hub over, the speed check passing, the readings on the hub's card, on Sensors and as the coolant line, the lights-off shortcut while DeadWare holds the hub, **Quit DeadWare** handing the hub back and the next start taking it again, sleep and wake, and switching the hub off. Those sittings found four faults, which have been fixed since. The build with the fixes has not yet been tried on a real PC, and three things in particular are still to be seen there: OpenRGB finding every other light while DeadWare drives the hub, a take-over that starts with a "before" reading (so that the speed comparison really runs), and the hub's lights returning to OpenRGB after the switch is turned off. Covered by tests only: iCUE starting while DeadWare drives the hub, a failed speed check, the stopped-pump rule, a stuck USB call, Retire with DeadLight running, a hub with unknown parts.
- **Going back to 0.22.0.** While the switch is on, and also after DeadWare has given the hub back (when the switch shows off), `lighting.json` is saved in a newer form (version 5), which 0.22.0 reads and runs the lights from but won't save lighting changes over. Before going back, switch the hub off if it is on, or choose **Back to OpenRGB, as before** if DeadWare gave it back; either saves the file in a form 0.22.0 can save over. Whenever DeadWare stops it turns OpenRGB's setting for the hub back on, but if 0.22.0 shows an unlit hub (the helper was ended, not stopped), start and quit 0.23.0 once.
