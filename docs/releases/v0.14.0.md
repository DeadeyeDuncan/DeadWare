- Add **Application resources** to Usage history: a card under Applications ranks the apps that used the most CPU time, memory or I/O in the Day, Week or Month shown, with a search box, a share bar of the period total and an **Everything else** row. The table shows CPU time, Avg CPU, Avg memory while listed, I/O read, I/O written and Minutes listed. Select an app to chart it across the period in the ranked metric; minutes in which it was not among the busiest are gaps, not zeros.
- Add **click a moment**: click a point on the Device CPU or RAM chart to list that slot's busiest apps under Application resources, ranked by CPU time from the CPU chart and by average memory from the RAM chart. On the keyboard, Left and Right move between points and Enter lists one. The card scrolls into view and a hairline marks the slot on both charts; **Clear** or Esc returns to the whole period.
- This is the saved process history the 0.13.0 notes said would come in 0.14.0. It has no switch of its own: it records while **Per-process monitoring** and **Record usage history on this device** are both on, and turning either off stops it and keeps what was saved until it is 30 days old. Settings, the Usage history page and the Processes page's off state now say so.
- Each minute keeps the top 10 apps by CPU time, by memory and by I/O (at most 30 apps) and one Everything else row that holds the rest of the apps the scan saw, in `process-history.db`, for 30 days under a 128 MiB cap that drops the oldest whole days first. Older days are removed when DeadWare starts and once a day, also while recording is off, and the card never shows them. A full day at the 30-app cap measured 3.4 MiB, so 30 such days fit under the cap.
- The card says how many minutes of the period have no process data (monitoring off, DeadWare not running, or a capture gap). Minutes from before the 30 days are not counted as gaps; a period that reaches back that far says they are not kept. A minute is saved after it ends, so the newest one appears a minute or so later.
- Known limits: usage by processes that exit between scans (very short-lived processes, and a process's last moments before it exits) is not recorded, so totals can be lower than the Device charts. CPU time and I/O in a saved minute are always numbers; when an app's counters cannot be compared for one scan, which is rare, that scan counts as 0, so a saved minute can understate an app rather than mark it unknown.
- App names (image names, never program paths) are saved for 30 days in `process-history.db` in this Windows profile and never uploaded, as Usage history already saves network per app. Diagnostics are unchanged.
