## 0.19.0 — Lighting comes to DeadWare

- **DeadWare runs your RGB lighting.** DeadLight's lighting is now part of DeadWare, so monitoring and lighting share one app. DeadWare drives the lights through OpenRGB, which you install yourself: it isn't included, and DeadWare is tested with OpenRGB 1.0rc3. Lighting is off until you turn it on, and nothing is brought over from DeadLight, so colours are set up afresh. This release has one colour for every zone, colours for single zones, **Lights on** and brightness; DeadLight's desk effects, keyboard and mouse controls and its other features aren't in it. While lighting is on, OpenRGB keeps a Corsair iCUE LINK System Hub in software mode, so the hub's fans and pump run at fixed speeds instead of its stored cooling curves, as under DeadLight; DeadWare doesn't set fan or pump speeds yet, and turning lighting off or quitting doesn't hand the hub back to its curves.
- **A Lighting page.** **Lighting**, below Cooling Health in the sidebar, has **Lights on** and **Brightness** in its header and a status line that says what lighting is doing or what stops it. **Set all colours** gives every zone one colour, with **Undo** while its picker is open, and each device's card lists its zones, so one zone can have a colour of its own. DeadWare switches Corsair's Lighting Node Pro to its built-in Rainbow Wave effect, because direct colours don't show properly on it, sizes its channel 1 when OpenRGB reports no LEDs there, as DeadLight does, and its card says so.
- **Settings → Lighting.** Choose `OpenRGB.exe` with **Browse…**, then turn on **Enable lighting**. A DeadWare running as administrator runs OpenRGB as administrator too, so it won't run OpenRGB from a folder that programs without administrator rights can change; Settings warns about such a folder and recommends copying OpenRGB to `C:\Program Files\OpenRGB`. OpenRGB's SDK server listens only on this PC. Its **Conflicts** list shows the programs that would fight DeadWare over the lights.
- **A lighting helper keeps the lights going.** A new background program in DeadWare's folder, `DeadWare.Devices.exe`, starts OpenRGB, sets the colours and saves your lighting choices. The lights stay on if DeadWare's window crashes, and the next start with the same administrator rights reconnects to the helper. DeadWare restarts the helper after an unexpected exit, never sooner than 4 seconds and less often each time, and gives up after five exits in ten minutes; the OpenRGB a helper started ends with it. **Quit DeadWare** stops the helper and ends its OpenRGB, except that a DeadWare without administrator rights can't stop a helper that has them (see the user guide). RAM, motherboard and graphics-card lighting need DeadWare to run as administrator.
- **Updates wait for the lighting helper.** **Update & restart** and **Roll back & restart** stop the helper first, and the updater replaces files only once it has exited. If it is still running after 10 seconds, the update stops, no files are replaced and DeadWare starts again as it was.
- **Programs that would fight over the lights are caught.** Two programs driving the same lights make them flicker. DeadWare doesn't start lighting while DeadLight, another OpenRGB, iCUE or a Corsair device service is running, and says which one and how to stop it. It checks every 5 seconds and starts lighting once they have gone; if one starts while DeadWare's lighting runs, DeadWare stops its OpenRGB until that program has gone. DeadWare only reports these programs and never changes their services or startup entries.
