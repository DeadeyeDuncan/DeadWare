## 0.19.0 — Lighting comes to DeadWare

- **DeadWare runs your RGB lighting.** DeadLight's lighting is now part of DeadWare, so monitoring and lighting share one app. DeadWare drives the lights through OpenRGB, which you install yourself: it isn't included, and DeadWare is tested with OpenRGB 1.0rc3. Lighting is off until you turn it on, and nothing is brought over from DeadLight, so colours are set up afresh. This release has one colour for every zone, colours for single zones, **Lights on**, brightness, and three desk effects, named after DeadLight's, with a desk layout (below); DeadLight's keyboard and mouse controls and its other features aren't in it. While lighting is on, OpenRGB keeps a Corsair iCUE LINK System Hub in software mode, so the hub's fans and pump run at fixed speeds, as they already do under DeadLight, rather than on the hub's stored cooling curves; DeadWare doesn't set fan or pump speeds yet, and turning lighting off or quitting doesn't hand the hub back to its curves.
- **A Lighting page.** **Lighting**, below Cooling Health in the sidebar, has **Lights on** and **Brightness** in its header and a status line that says what lighting is doing or what stops it. **Set all colours** gives every zone one colour, with **Undo** while its picker is open. Select a device on the desk or in the device list and its panel lists its zones, so one zone can have a colour of its own; a device running its own effect, or one DeadWare can't drive, lists none. DeadWare switches Corsair's Lighting Node Pro to its built-in Rainbow Wave effect, because direct colours don't show properly on it, and its panel says so. When OpenRGB reports no LEDs on the Node Pro's channel 1, DeadWare sizes it, as DeadLight does.
- **Desk effects.** Four tiles choose the effect: **Static**, your saved colours; **Wave**, which sweeps across the desk with up to 8 colours of your choice, or the full rainbow with none, and its own **Speed**, **Direction** (one of eight) and **Spread**; **Breathing**, where every zone pulses its own colour (the shared colour for a zone without one) in step; and **Spectrum**, where everything moves round the full colour circle together. A choice applies at once and each effect keeps its own settings. Effects run in the lighting helper, so they keep going with the window closed. A device on its own effect, such as the Lighting Node Pro, keeps it under every effect, and a device taken off the desk keeps its saved colour. Starting an effect can take up to about half a second.
- **A picture of the desk.** On a PC with an MSI MAG B650M MORTAR WIFI motherboard and a Corsair iCUE LINK System Hub, the **Desk** card shows a built-in drawing of the desk with the keyboard, the mouse and the PC tower. Tap the tower to see inside: radiator fans 1 to 3, the pump, RAM 1 and 2, the graphics card and the front fans; **← Back to desk** returns. The picture grows with the window up to 1280 px wide, with the colour card beside it, which reads "Nothing selected" until you tap a part and names its rows after the parts. A part whose device isn't found is marked "not found", and the picture is dimmed while the lighting helper isn't running. The drawing is one tab stop: the arrow keys move through the parts, Enter or Space on the tower opens the inside view and Escape goes back to the desk. While Wave runs, the desk view shows a band and an arrow for its direction. A PC with the picture can't rearrange its layout, so **Edit layout**, the device list and **Not placed** aren't shown. Any PC with that motherboard and hub gets this picture, and there is no switch back to the plain desk yet. The settings under the effect tiles (**Colours**, **Speed**, **Direction**, **Spread**) now sit on one row where the window allows.
- **A desk layout.** On any other PC, the **Desk** card draws your desk from above with a box for each lit device and a PC tower that opens to show the devices inside it, and, while the Lighting page is showing, the colours the lights are taking, before **Brightness**. New devices are placed by themselves, so effects work before you arrange anything. Under the desk, a device list says where each device is (On desk, Inside PC, Own effect, Not placed, or Not on desk for one DeadWare can't light) and offers **Place on desk** for devices you took off it. **Edit layout** lets you drag devices and the tower, drop a device on the tower or out of it, move a device's zones separately, nudge with the arrow keys, choose a **Desk shape** and **Reset positions**; the lights follow a move when you drop it.
- **Settings → Lighting.** Choose `OpenRGB.exe` with **Browse…**, then turn on **Enable lighting**. A DeadWare running as administrator runs OpenRGB as administrator too, so it won't run OpenRGB from a folder that programs without administrator rights can change; Settings warns about such a folder and recommends copying OpenRGB to `C:\Program Files\OpenRGB`. Its **Conflicts** list shows the programs that would fight DeadWare over the lights. OpenRGB's SDK server listens only on this PC, and while DeadWare runs as administrator only its lighting helper can connect to the OpenRGB it starts; other programs, other RGB apps included, can't use it. The OpenRGB that DeadWare starts keeps its settings and its log in a folder of DeadWare's, apart from those of an OpenRGB you run yourself.
- **A lighting helper keeps the lights going.** A new background program in DeadWare's folder, `DeadWare.Devices.exe`, starts OpenRGB, sets the colours and saves your lighting choices. The lights stay on if DeadWare's window crashes, and the next start with the same administrator rights reconnects to the helper. DeadWare restarts the helper after an unexpected exit, never sooner than 4 seconds and less often each time, and gives up after five exits in ten minutes; the OpenRGB a helper started ends with it, unless Windows refused to tie the two together, which the helper's log records, or the helper died in the instant between starting OpenRGB and tying it. **Quit DeadWare** stops the helper and ends its OpenRGB, except that a DeadWare without administrator rights can't stop a helper that has them (see the user guide). RAM, motherboard and graphics-card lighting need DeadWare to run as administrator.
- **Updates wait for the lighting helper.** **Update & restart** and **Roll back & restart** stop the helper first, and the updater replaces files only once it has exited. If it is still running after 10 seconds, the update stops, no files are replaced and DeadWare starts again as it was.
- **Programs that would fight over the lights are caught.** Two programs driving the same lights make them flicker. DeadWare doesn't start lighting while DeadLight, another OpenRGB, iCUE or a Corsair device service is running, and says which one and how to stop it. It checks every 5 seconds and starts lighting once they have gone; if one starts while DeadWare's lighting runs, DeadWare stops its OpenRGB until that program has gone. Each such stop counts toward the same give-up as OpenRGB stopping unexpectedly, five in ten minutes, and after a give-up only turning lighting off and on in Settings starts OpenRGB again. DeadWare only reports these programs and never changes their services or startup entries.
