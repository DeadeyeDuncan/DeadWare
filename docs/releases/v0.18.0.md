## 0.18.0 — A native Windows interface

- **Rebuilt on WinUI 3.** DeadWare's interface now uses Windows' own WinUI 3 controls. Every page, the pop-out graphs, the desktop widget, the tray icon, Start with Windows and the updater carry over, and your settings, histories and window positions are used as they are. Light or dark mode and the accent colour follow your Windows settings, with the Mica backdrop on Windows 11.
- **Graphs slide.** Live graphs scroll continuously between readings instead of stepping once per poll, and the newest sample glides in at the right edge.
- **Themes and Window glass are gone.** The nine colour themes, including the Elder Scrolls themes with their lettering and runes, and **Window glass** are not part of this interface. Your saved theme and glass choice stay in the settings file but have no effect.
- **Graph colours and line styles moved to Settings.** The colour circles in each Overview graph's corner are replaced by **Settings → Appearance → Graph lines**: pick a trace, then its colour, pattern, thickness and glow. Colours and styles you saved before still apply.
- **Storage shows every drive at once.** Each drive has its own card with its read and write rates, their statistics for the selected range, its chart, volumes and health, instead of a list with one drive selected.
- **Per-app network rates no longer drop to zero on every other poll.** Windows hands over network trace events about once a second, so a poll could find none and show 0 B/s for every app. DeadWare now asks for the pending events at each poll.
- **The network total counts each adapter once.** Windows lists extra filter-layer copies of an adapter with the same counters, and the Network total added them all, reading several times the real rate on some PCs.
- **Storage health reads correctly.** Healthy drives could show **Failing** or **Warning**, and SATA drives could show no power-on hours. A drive whose health the hardware library cannot judge, such as some NVMe drives in USB enclosures, now reads **Unknown** instead of OK.
- **Windows 10 needs version 2004 or later.** Windows 11 is unaffected.
- **Updating from 0.17.** **Updates → Update & restart** replaces the app in place. The installed folder grows from about 230 MB to about 300 MB. A few empty folders from the old layout (language folders such as `de` and `fr`) are left behind; they are harmless and can be deleted.
- **Returning to 0.17.13.** **Updates → Roll back & restart** restores the previous installation. 0.18.0 changes no history file, and it writes settings and window positions in the format 0.17 reads, so the older app opens with your themes, layout and histories as they were.
