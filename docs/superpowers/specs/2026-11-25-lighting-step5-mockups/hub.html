5 · What DeadWare does with the hub, moment by moment (nothing to look at)

Lights off: the switch, the shortcut, idle or lockThe hub's lights go dark. DeadWare keeps hold of the hub, so nothing blinks and the readings carry on.
Quit DeadWare, lighting switched off, or Update & restartDeadWare first gives the hub back to its own built-in mode. The hub then shows its own stored light effect and runs its own stored fan settings until DeadWare starts again. Today nothing gives it back.
The PC sleepsDeadWare sends nothing. After waking it waits about 10 seconds, as today, then takes the hub again and repaints it.
iCUE or a Corsair service startsDeadWare lets go of the hub at once and says why. When they are gone it takes the hub again by itself.
DeadWare's window crashesNothing changes: the background helper keeps driving the hub, as it keeps the other lights on today.
The background helper itself diesThe hub is expected to fall back to its own mode after about 10 seconds of silence. That is one of the things to confirm on your PC. DeadWare restarts the helper, which takes the hub again.
Going back to 0.22DeadWare hands the hub's lights back to OpenRGB whenever it stops, so the older version finds things as it expects.

Choices I made for you (say a number to change one)

  1. The switch is in Settings → Lighting and is off after the update. Switching it on always asks first and shows the hub's readings. Switching it off gives the hub's lights back to OpenRGB. Either way all lights blink once, because OpenRGB has to restart.
  2. The check after the take-over: more than 10% slower means stop. DeadWare compares pump and fan speeds from just before with those about 15 seconds after. If any is more than 10% slower, it gives the hub back to its own built-in mode, switches itself off and waits for you to choose: back to OpenRGB, or try again.
  3. A stopped pump also makes DeadWare let go. While it drives the hub, if the pump reads 0 RPM for about 10 seconds, DeadWare gives the hub back to its own built-in mode and says so. It does not take it again by itself.
  4. Dark is not the same as let go. Lights off, idle and lock make the hub dark while DeadWare keeps hold. Only Quit, lighting off, an update, iCUE appearing or a failed check hand the hub back.
  5. Each fan and the pump stay one colour each, as today, and your hub colours are carried over once. Lighting single lights inside a fan is not part of this step.
  6. Readings show in three places: the hub's card, five rows on Sensors, and the optional coolant line. They exist only while DeadWare drives the hub, so the coolant line needs lighting on and the switch on.
  7. The coolant line starts unticked, like the pump and GPU fan lines, and is saved in the 6-hour graph history like the others.
  8. Cooling Health is left as it is. It keeps using the motherboard's pump reading and does not use coolant temperature yet; its wording is corrected to say that.
  9. iCUE running, or a Corsair service running, blocks the take-over (red mark). "Starts with Windows" alone is a warning (yellow mark): DeadWare would simply let go at the next sign-in until it is gone.
  10. Retire DeadLight switches off its start-up task and stops it, and deletes nothing. The button shows only when there is something to retire, and needs DeadWare running as administrator. On your PC today there is nothing left to retire.
  11. A hub with a part DeadWare doesn't know (another fan or pump model it can't identify for certain) is refused: the switch stays off, with the reason, and the hub stays on OpenRGB.
  12. Not included: a third position, "leave the hub alone". That would let the hub run its own stored fan settings and lights all the time, with DeadWare not lighting it. Say if you want it; my advice is to wait for fan control instead.
  13. This would be version 0.23.0.
  14. The front three fans are not part of this step. They are Corsair too, but they plug into a different Corsair box, the Lighting Node Pro, not the iCUE LINK hub. They stay as today: lit through OpenRGB, on their own Rainbow Wave. That box only lights the fans; it does not set their speeds.
